Congestion Control

A team study comparing TCP CUBIC and TCP BBR when many senders transmit to one receiver at the same time. We examined how FIFO, RED and FqCoDel affected goodput, flow-completion time, packet loss and fairness.

Study Question

How does queue management affect CUBIC and BBR during incast?

Incast occurs when many senders transmit to one receiver at nearly the same time. The burst can overwhelm a bottleneck queue, causing packet loss, retransmissions and longer transfer times.

We first varied sender count and queue depth under FIFO. We then used a fixed stress case with 64 senders and a configured queue depth of 50 packets to compare two TCP variants, each paired with three queue disciplines.

Method

Compare configurations under a controlled workload.

We examined aggregate performance and per-flow behaviour to understand the trade-offs between transfer rate, completion time, loss and fairness.

01

Create synchronized traffic

Used synchronized BulkSend applications in ns-3 to generate a many-to-one burst through the bottleneck.

02

Vary the initial conditions

Tested sender counts from 2 to 64 and queue depths from 10 to 100 packets to examine when performance deteriorated.

03

Compare six configurations

Paired CUBIC and BBR with FIFO, RED and FqCoDel under the same 64-sender stress condition.

04

Analyse the trade-offs

Compared aggregate goodput, median flow-completion time, packet loss and Jain’s fairness index.

Simulation Results

Different pairings favoured different metrics.

In the tested workload, CUBIC with FqCoDel had the highest goodput and fairness index. BBR with FIFO or RED had the lowest median flow-completion time.

Interpretation and Limits

No single configuration led on every metric.

The results showed why congestion control and queue management need to be considered together. In this experiment, the configuration with the highest goodput did not have the shortest median completion time.

These findings describe the simulated topology, traffic pattern and settings used in the study. Other workloads or network configurations would need separate testing.

01
Interactions matter

FqCoDel increased goodput and fairness for CUBIC, while the BBR pairing produced lower goodput and higher loss under the tested conditions.

02
Use several measures

Goodput alone did not describe the full result. Completion time, loss and fairness revealed additional trade-offs. The highest fairness index was 0.51, still below the ideal value of 1.

03
Keep the model’s limits visible

Perfectly synchronized senders created a deliberately demanding test case. The study used FqCoDel as its fair-queueing approach; it was not an exact implementation of weighted fair queueing.
