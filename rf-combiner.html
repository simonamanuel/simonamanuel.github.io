Project Overview

Exploring a wider operating bandwidth.

This project compared single-stage and multi-stage Wilkinson designs, with a focus on matching and isolation across a specified frequency band. I used calculations and simulation to develop the circuit, then translated the transmission-line impedances into microstrip dimensions.

Design decision

Use three matching stages to explore the bandwidth trade-off.

The single-stage design provided a simpler starting point. The three-stage structure added more parameters to tune, allowing the response to be adjusted across a wider band. I compared the simulated responses before developing the microstrip model.

  • Compared single-stage and multi-stage responses
  • Tuned impedance matching and output-port isolation
  • Cross-checked microstrip widths in TXLINE and AWRDE
  • Recorded the resistor-footprint limitation in the layout
My Work

From initial calculations to a microstrip model.

The work combined analytical calculations, circuit comparisons and simulation-based refinement.

01

Initial calculations

Used analytical calculations, MATLAB and TXLINE to establish starting transmission-line parameters and check the calculated dimensions.

02

Design comparisons

Compared single-stage and multi-stage matching structures to examine how the number of sections affected bandwidth and the simulated response.

03

Circuit optimization

Used AWRDE to tune circuit parameters and evaluate return loss and isolation across the target frequency range.

04

Microstrip modelling

Converted the ideal transmission lines into microstrip dimensions on Rogers RO4003 and evaluated the resulting model in simulation.
