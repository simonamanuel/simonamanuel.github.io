Study Question

Transport congestion control under synchronized incast traffic.

Incast produces near-simultaneous many-to-one TCP bursts. Bottleneck queue saturation increases packet loss, retransmission pressure, and tail completion times; the outcome depends on both transport congestion control and active queue management.

A baseline FIFO sweep varied sender counts and queue depths. The controlled stress experiment then compared six CUBIC/BBR × FIFO/RED/FqCoDel combinations at 64 senders and a configured 50-packet queue.

Method

Reproducible simulation design and performance metrics.

The experiment tracked end-to-end transfer performance and per-flow allocation to expose throughput, latency, loss, and fairness trade-offs.

01

Create synchronized traffic

Generated synchronized many-to-one application traffic using ns-3 BulkSend clients and a shared bottleneck link.

02

Vary the initial conditions

Swept 2 to 64 senders and bottleneck queue capacities of 10 to 100 packets to characterize congestion sensitivity.

03

Compare six configurations

Evaluated the six congestion-control/queue-discipline pairings under a common 64-sender, 50-packet workload.

04

Analyse the trade-offs

Computed aggregate application goodput, median flow-completion time, packet loss, and Jain’s fairness index for each configuration.

Simulation Results

No single TCP/AQM pairing dominated every metric.

Under the tested workload, CUBIC/FqCoDel achieved the highest aggregate goodput and fairness; BBR/FIFO and BBR/RED produced the shortest median flow-completion times.

Interpretation and Limits

Throughput, latency and fairness trade-offs in incast.

The best-performing configuration depended on the metric: maximizing goodput did not minimize median completion time. Transport control and bottleneck queue management therefore cannot be evaluated independently.

Results are specific to the modeled topology, offered load, traffic synchronization, and queue parameters; other operating regimes require separate experiments.

01
Interactions matter

For CUBIC, FqCoDel improved aggregate goodput and fairness in the stress case, while its BBR pairing showed greater loss and lower goodput.

02
Use several measures

The maximum observed Jain fairness index was 0.51, well below the ideal value of 1; goodput, completion time, and loss provide necessary complementary evidence.

03
Keep the model’s limits visible

The workload intentionally synchronized senders to stress the bottleneck. FqCoDel supplied fair queueing with controlled delay; it was not an implementation of weighted fair queueing.
